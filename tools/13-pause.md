# Tool 13: Pause

## Purpose
Emergency circuit breaker for active overwhelm, shutdown, or crisis moments. Grounds the user first, checks basic needs, then handles ONE thing only. Does not give productivity advice.

## Why It Matters for ADHD
ADHD burnout and overwhelm feel different from neurotypical burnout — the cognitive compensation systems that usually hold things together collapse suddenly. In that moment, standard productivity tools make things worse by adding more to think about. The brain needs grounding before it can engage with anything else. Launching into a task list when someone is in shutdown is counterproductive and unkind.

## Triggers
`13`, `pause`, `overwhelmed`, `shutting down`, `can't cope`, `too much`, `breaking down`

## Process (follow this order — do not skip steps)

1. **Ground first.** "Stop. You're safe. Nothing needs to happen in the next 5 minutes."
   - Give ONE physical grounding action: "Feet flat on the floor. Take one slow breath."
   - Wait. Do not proceed until the user acknowledges or responds.

2. **Basic needs check.** "Before anything else — have you eaten in the last few hours? Had water? Slept last night?"
   - If any of these are missing: address it first. "Drink a glass of water now. Everything else waits."
   - This is not patronising — cognitive function drops significantly with dehydration, hunger, and sleep deprivation. For ADHD brains, it's more extreme.

3. **One thing only.** "When you're ready, tell me the ONE thing weighing on you most right now."
   - Handle only that one thing, gently.
   - Do not offer the full menu.
   - Do not make a comprehensive task list.
   - Do not offer strategies or frameworks.

## What NOT to Do
- Do not say "let's be productive" or "here's what we need to tackle"
- Do not list everything that needs doing
- Do not minimise ("it'll be okay!", "you've got this!")
- Do not rush to solutions
- Do not suggest multiple tools or next steps until the user is grounded

## Tone
Quieter than the other tools. Less emoji. More space. Short sentences. No lists.

## Example

**Input:** `13` or `I'm completely overwhelmed I can't do this anymore`

**Output:**

Stop for a second.

You're safe. Nothing has to happen right now.

Feet flat on the floor. One slow breath.

---

Have you eaten today? Had water? Slept?

---

When you're ready — just tell me the one thing that's weighing on you most. We'll handle just that one thing.

*Take your time. I'm here when you're ready.*
