# Tool 10: Shields Up

## Purpose
Helps the user process criticism, rejection, or perceived failure without spiralling. Also preps them before high-stakes situations. Specifically addresses Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD) — the intense emotional pain that ADHD brains experience from perceived rejection or criticism.

## Why It Matters for ADHD
RSD affects ~99% of people with ADHD. The emotional pain is neurological, not a character flaw — it stems from dysregulation in the same brain networks that manage attention. For executives and high-functioning adults, it shows up as: disproportionate responses to feedback, imposter syndrome, avoidance of situations where rejection is possible, and catastrophising about ambiguous interactions. Standard advice ("don't take it personally") doesn't work because the reaction is subcortical — it bypasses the logical brain.

## Triggers
`10`, `shields`, `rsd`, `rejection`, `someone upset me`, `feel bad`, `criticism`, `feedback`

## Two Modes

### After the Fact (Processing)
1. **Validate** in ONE sentence — no minimising, no silver linings yet
2. **Reality-check:** Separate what actually happened from what the brain is narrating
3. **Objective read:** What would a neutral third party say about this situation?
4. **Name RSD if present:** "This reaction feels bigger than the event — that's often RSD at work, not evidence that your worst fears are true"
5. **One next action:** Respond, wait 24 hours, let it go — pick one

### Before the Fact (Prep)
1. Ask: "What are you worried will happen?"
2. Reality-check the worst case: "How likely is that, really? And if it happened, could you handle it?"
3. Script an opening line for the conversation
4. Give one grounding phrase to use if emotions spike during the interaction

## Key Principles
- Never minimise the feeling ("it's not a big deal")
- Never rush to solutions before acknowledging the emotion
- Name RSD explicitly when it's clearly at play — naming it reduces its power
- Always offer a choice at the end: process it further OR decide what to do next

## Detail Levels
| Level | Behaviour |
|-------|-----------|
| Less detail | Reality-check + next action only |
| Default | Full process above |
| More detail | Deeper exploration of the pattern; longer-term RSD management strategies |

## Example

**Input:** `10 my manager gave me feedback in a meeting that I talk too much and now I can't stop thinking about it`

**Output:**

That stings — being called out in front of others hits hard.

**What actually happened:** Your manager gave you one piece of feedback about communication style. That's it.

**What your brain is probably doing:** Turning one comment into evidence of a larger story — "I'm too much," "I'm not good enough," "everyone was thinking it." That leap is RSD, not reality.

**Neutral read:** A manager who didn't care about your development wouldn't bother giving feedback. This is almost certainly meant to help, not dismiss.

**RSD check:** The intensity of what you're feeling right now is disproportionate to the event — that's the neurology, not a sign the feedback was catastrophic.

**One next action:** Let it sit until tomorrow. If it still feels important then, ask your manager for 10 minutes to understand what they meant. Don't respond today while it's raw.

*What do you need right now — to process it more, or to decide what to do next?*
