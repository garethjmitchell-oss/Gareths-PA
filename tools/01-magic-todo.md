# Tool 1: Magic ToDo

## Purpose
Breaks an overwhelming task into bite-sized, actionable steps. The flagship tool — the one most ADHD users reach for first.

## Why It Matters for ADHD
Task initiation paralysis is one of the most common ADHD struggles. A big task feels like a wall. Breaking it into small, concrete steps gives the brain a way in. Each step should feel so small that starting it requires almost no willpower.

## Triggers
`1`, `todo`, `break down`, `steps`

## Input
A task of any size. Can be vague ("clean the house") or specific ("prepare a presentation for the Monday team meeting about Q3 results").

## Output Format
- Numbered checklist with empty checkboxes: `- [ ]`
- Each step is ONE concrete, physical action
- Steps are specific ("Wipe down kitchen counters with a damp cloth") not vague ("Clean kitchen")
- The easiest/quickest step gets a star marker
- If the list exceeds 10 items, add: "Start with just the first 3"

## Detail Levels
| Level | Behaviour |
|-------|-----------|
| Less detail | 3-5 high-level steps |
| Default | 5-8 steps, some with brief sub-steps |
| More detail | 10+ granular steps, every micro-action spelled out |

## Recursive Breakdown
The user can reply with a step number (e.g., "3") to break that step into sub-steps. This can go as deep as needed.

## ADHD Adaptations
- First step is always a physical, concrete action (not abstract)
- ⭐ marks the quickest win — reorder steps so it comes first for momentum
- Long lists get a "start with the first 3" note to prevent overwhelm
- In "just start" mode, steps are delivered one at a time
- **Wall of Awful check:** If the task sounds emotionally loaded (phone calls, difficult admin, long-avoided tasks), acknowledge it: "This one has some weight to it — let's make it as small as possible." Suggest a dopamine primer before step 1.
- **Dopamine primer suggestion:** "Put on music or a podcast, then tackle step 1."

## Example

**Input:** `1 do my taxes`

**Output:**
- [ ] 1. **Grab your laptop** and sit at a table (not the couch)
- [ ] 2. Open your email and **search for tax documents** (P60, P45, bank statements)
- [ ] 3. **Download** any documents you find to one folder on your desktop
- [ ] 4. Go to the HMRC website and **log in** to your tax account
- [ ] 5. Start a new return and **fill in the employment section** using your P60
- [ ] 6. Fill in the **interest/savings section** using your bank statements
- [ ] 7. **Review the summary** and check the numbers look right
- [ ] 8. **Submit** and save the confirmation

*Break down any step? Say its number.*
