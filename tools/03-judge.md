# Tool 3: Judge

## Purpose
Analyses the emotional tone and vibe of a message. Tells you if something sounds rude, passive-aggressive, friendly, or neutral — so you don't have to guess.

## Why It Matters for ADHD
Many neurodivergent people struggle to read emotional subtext in written communication. "Is this person annoyed at me?" is an exhausting question to sit with. This tool gives a clear, objective read so you can stop spiralling and respond with confidence.

## Triggers
`3`, `judge`, `vibe`, `tone check`, `how does this sound`

## Input
A message — either something the user received or something they wrote and want to check before sending.

## Output Format
- **Lead with the verdict** — a tone label in 1-3 words with an emoji
  - Examples: "Friendly but rushed", "Passive-aggressive", "Warm and genuine", "Neutral / business-like"
- 2-3 specific observations explaining WHY it reads that way
- If the user wrote it: flag anything that might land differently than intended
- If ambiguous: "Could be read as... but more likely..."

## Detail Levels
| Level | Behaviour |
|-------|-----------|
| Less detail | Just the tone label, one line |
| Default | Tone label + 2-3 observations |
| More detail | Line-by-line analysis with specific phrases flagged |

## Example

**Input:** `3 "Per my last email, the deadline was Friday. Please advise on next steps."`

**Output:**

**Tone: Passive-aggressive / frustrated**

- "Per my last email" signals they've already said this and are annoyed you didn't act on it
- "Please advise" is formally polite but cold — creates distance
- The full stop after "Friday" makes it feel blunt and final

This person is probably irritated but keeping it professional. They want action, not an apology.

## RSD Filter
Always include after the analysis: "Note — your ADHD brain may be reading this as more hostile or critical than it is. Here's the most neutral interpretation: [restate charitably]."

This is not optional. RSD causes ADHD brains to over-interpret tone as negative. The charitable reframe is often more accurate than the initial read.
